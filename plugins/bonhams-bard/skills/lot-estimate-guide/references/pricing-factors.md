# Pricing factors

Weigh these when adjusting from comparables to the subject piece.

## Drives value up

- Prestigious signature (Cartier, Van Cleef & Arpels, Bulgari, Tiffany and similar)
- Exceptional stone quality: colour, clarity, cut
- Large individual stones; price per carat rises sharply above certain weights
- Rare colours and origins: pigeon-blood ruby, cornflower or Kashmir sapphire, vivid yellow or fancy-colour diamond, Colombian emerald
- Absence of heating or treatment, confirmed by a recognised laboratory
- Documented provenance, original box and papers
- Highly collected periods (Art Deco and similar)
- A strong recent market for the category, shown by recent results beating estimates

## Drives value down

- Unsigned or maker unknown
- Lower colour or clarity grades
- Condition issues: chips, missing stones, worn enamel, replaced parts
- Unfashionable period or style
- Heavy metal weight relative to stone quality, unless the metal is notable
- A soft recent market for the category

## Made by, or cased by

A maker's mark means the house made the piece. A maker's case alone means it was retailed there and may come from another workshop, which usually lowers the price. Check before relying on a signed comparable.

## Loose stones

Laboratory reports (GIA, Gübelin, SSEF, and similar) strongly affect buyer confidence and price. Flag whether one is present. As at October 2026, about 47% of Bonhams loose stones sold in FY26 hammered below the low estimate (median hammer about 1.0 times the low, against 1.1 to 1.3 for most other types), so check the type's current record in `bard_home` before settling a range.

## Watches

BARD covers jewellery sales, so use the four house results pages for watches. Additional factors: brand and reference number, movement type, case material, box and papers (these change value dramatically), service history.
